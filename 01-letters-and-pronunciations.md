# Alphabets

## Consonants

```
b c d f g j k l m n p r s t v x z '
```

## Vowels

```
a e i o u y
```

## Notes

- Lojban does not use `h`, `w`, or `q`.
- Lojban uses `'` (a single quote, apostrophe, or hacek) to represent the **voiced glottal fricative** /h/ sound, similar to the English "h" in "house." This symbol was chosen to minimize confusion and maintain a clear distinction between Lojban letters and sounds.
- `y` is always a vowel in Lojban and represents the schwa sound /ə/.

# Pronunciations

## Consonants

Most consonants are pronounced as in English:

- **b** = /b/ as in "boy"
- **d** = /d/ as in "dog"
- **f** = /f/ as in "fish"
- **g** = /ɡ/ as in "go"
- **k** = /k/ as in "kite"
- **l** = /l/ as in "leaf"
- **m** = /m/ as in "moon"
- **n** = /n/ as in "net"
- **p** = /p/ as in "pen"
- **r** = /r/ as in "red" (trilled or tapped /ɾ/ in some dialects)
- **s** = /s/ as in "see"
- **t** = /t/ as in "top"
- **v** = /v/ as in "voice"
- **z** = /z/ as in "zoo"

With Exceptions being:

- **c** = /ʃ/ as in "**sh**ell"
- **j** = /ʒ/ as in "mea**s**ure" or "plea**s**ure"
- **x** = /χ/ as in "ba**ch**" or "lo**ch**" (voiceless uvular fricative)
- **'** = /h/ (English `h`) as in "**h**ouse" (voiced glottal fricative)

## Vowels

- **a** = /a/ as in "f**a**ther" (not "hat")
- **e** = /ɛ/ as in "g**e**t"
- **i** = /i/ as in "f**ee**t" or "mach**i**ne"
- **o** = /o/ as in "b**o**ld" or "m**o**re" (pure sound)
- **u** = /u/ as in "c**oo**l"
- **y** = /ə/ (schwa sound) as in "sof**a**"

# Schwa Sound

In Lojban, the letter `y` represents the schwa sound, which is a short, neutral vowel sound pronounced as /ə/ in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA). It is one of the most common vowel sounds in English and many other languages.

## Why is `y` Called the Schwa Sound in Lojban?

1. **Phonetic Neutrality:** The schwa sound /ə/ is a neutral vowel, often occurring in unstressed syllables. In Lojban, `y` serves as a "filler" vowel to break up clusters of consonants that are difficult to pronounce without adding a specific vowel sound. This use makes it phonetically neutral, similar to how the schwa functions in natural languages.

2. **Distinct Representation:** Lojban uses `a`, `e`, `i`, `o`, and `u` for specific vowel sounds. Using `y` for the schwa /ə/ provides a clear distinction from these primary vowels and avoids confusion with their standard pronunciations.

## How is the Schwa Sound Pronounced?

- **Relaxed Tongue Position:** The tongue is placed in a relaxed, central position in the mouth.
- **Short and Unstressed:** It is pronounced briefly and without emphasis. It is the most common sound for unstressed syllables in English words.

## Examples in English:

- **About**: The first 'a' is pronounced as /ə/ (schwa), so it sounds like /əˈbaʊt/.
- **Syringe**: The 'y' is pronounced as a schwa /sɪˈrɪn(d)ʒ/, sounding like /səˈrɪn(d)ʒ/.
- **Taken**: The 'e' is pronounced as /təˈkeɪkən/.

In summary, Lojban uses `y` to represent the schwa sound /ə/ because it is a neutral, unstressed sound that helps maintain the language's phonetic consistency. The pronunciation is relaxed, central, and brief, making it distinct from the primary vowels.

# Diphthongs

A **diphthong** is a complex vowel sound that occurs when the tongue moves from one vowel position to another within the same syllable, creating a single, smooth gliding sound. The term comes from the Greek words "di-" meaning "two" and "phthongos" meaning "sound" or "tone," signifying a combination of two vowel sounds.

### Characteristics of Diphthongs

1. **Single Syllable Sound:** Although they involve two vowel qualities, diphthongs are produced as a single syllable.
2. **Gliding Movement:** The tongue glides from one vowel position to another during pronunciation.
3. **Two Vowel Components:** A diphthong typically starts with one vowel sound (the "onset") and glides toward another vowel sound (the "offset").

### Examples in English

1. **/aɪ/** as in "light" or "fly" (starts with the vowel sound /a/ and glides to /ɪ/).
2. **/eɪ/** as in "day" or "say" (starts with /e/ and glides to /ɪ/).
3. **/ɔɪ/** as in "boy" or "toy" (starts with /ɔ/ and glides to /ɪ/).
4. **/aʊ/** as in "now" or "how" (starts with /a/ and glides to /ʊ/).
5. **/oʊ/** as in "go" or "so" (starts with /o/ and glides to /ʊ/).

### Diphthongs vs. Monophthongs

- **Monophthong:** A single, unchanging vowel sound, like /i/ in "sit" or /a/ in "cat."
- **Diphthong:** A vowel sound that combines two distinct positions, as explained above.

### Importance in Languages

- Diphthongs add complexity and variety to the phonetic inventory of a language. They are crucial in distinguishing word meanings and pronunciations in many languages.

### In Summary

Diphthongs are essentially "vowel glides" within a single syllable, starting with one vowel sound and ending with another, creating a seamless transition that gives languages like English their characteristic sound patterns.
